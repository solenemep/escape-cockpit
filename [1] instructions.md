# Crossmint | Engineering Challenge: 3D Escape Room [External]

_Crossmint
Proprietary & Confidential_

### Overview

We want to see how you think, build, and ship, especially in territory that might be unfamiliar.

Your challenge is to build a **3D escape room that runs in the browser**. The theme, visual style, and story are entirely up to you. What matters is that it works, it's fun (or at least satisfying) to play, and the code behind it is something you'd be proud to show.

This is a take-home challenge. We know you have other commitments, so take the time you need. Most people spend around 2-3 hours of focused work on it, and it doesn't have to be in one sitting. We don't factor time into our evaluation, but the sooner you submit, the faster we can move forward together. Ship it when it feels ready.

---

### What to build

A browser-based 3D escape room with **exactly 4 puzzles** that the player must solve to escape.

You choose:

- The theme and setting
- The visual style (simple geometry is fine, photorealism is not required)
- The story or framing, if any
- The specific puzzles

What we require:

- It runs in a **desktop browser** with no installation (mobile support is not required)
- There are **4 distinct puzzles**, each gating progress to the next
- The player can actually **win** (reach an end state)
- A few **in-world hints** are available to the player; they shouldn't need to read source code to have a chance

---

### Puzzle design guidance

Each puzzle should feel different. To spark ideas, here are four puzzle archetypes you might consider (you're free to use these, mix them, or ignore them entirely):

1. **Observation:** the player notices something in the environment and acts on it
2. **Logic:** a sequence, pattern, or rule must be discovered and applied
3. **Interaction:** the player manipulates objects in the scene to trigger a change
4. **Combination:** clues gathered from earlier puzzles come together to unlock the final door

The puzzles don't need to be hard. They need to be clear, intentional, and solvable.

### Technology

Language and framework agnostic. The only requirement is that it runs in a desktop browser.

Use AI tools. We actively encourage it, and the ability to build well in unfamiliar territory with AI assistance is part of what we're evaluating. Briefly mention in your README how you used AI: what you delegated, what you kept, and where it helped or fell short.

---

### What we're looking for

We'll evaluate the submission across three areas:

- **The product:** Is it playable? Is it coherent? Does it work?
- **The code:** Is it AI slop, or actually properly architected code that could get inside a production environment? Would another developer be able to pick it up?
- **The approach:** Did you make good decisions under constraints? Were you creative in a domain that might be new to you?

We are not looking for a polished AAA game. We are looking for evidence that you can ship something real in a new domain, make smart trade-offs, and use AI effectively to write code.
